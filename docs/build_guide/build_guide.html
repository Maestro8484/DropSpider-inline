DropSpider build guide, Rev C.1

One page, top to bottom: wire the 30-pin ESP32 DevKit V1, assemble the device, power it up, then set it up and test it from its own web page. The repo docs (02, 05, 06, 07, 09) are the source of truth; if this page disagrees, the doc wins.

The board still runs the Rev C firmware. Until the task 1 update, do not press Drop it now or Release, and do not type drop or rel: they drop with the motor off, which Rev C.1 forbids.

01Parts and tools

Electrical parts (all already owned)

  • 30-pin ESP32 DevKit V1 (the 38-pin NodeMCU-32S works too, same GPIO numbers)
  • TMC2209 driver on the expansion board
  • NEMA 11 stepper motor, 4 wires
  • Micro servo (SG90 or MG90S)
  • KW12-3 roller micro switch
  • HLK-LD2450 radar with its 4-pin 1.25 mm cable
  • MP1584EN mini buck module, fixed 5 V out
  • 12 V 2 A adapter with screw-terminal jack
  • Dupont jumpers, 22 AWG wire for the 12 V run, heat shrink

Left out on purpose: the 470 uF servo capacitor, the 74AHCT125 buffer and the LD2410C backup radar. Each is only fitted if a named test fails.

Tools

  • Multimeter (needed before anything is connected)
  • Soldering iron, solder, heat gun or lighter for heat shrink
  • Small Phillips, small flat screwdriver (driver current pot)
  • 1.5 mm and 2 mm hex keys (coupler set screws)
  • Vise or clamp, CA glue, blue threadlocker
  • A phone or PC on the same WiFi as the board

02Board pinout, top view

Board face up, antenna end away from you, USB toward you. This is the 30-pin ESP32 DevKit V1: 15 pins a side, printed D25 for GPIO25 and so on, RX2/TX2 for GPIO16/17, VIN for the 5 V input. Go by the name printed next to the pin on your board, not by counting. On the 38-pin NodeMCU-32S the same GPIO numbers sit in other places.

03Power: one tree, no loops

12 V feeds the motor driver and the buck converter. The buck makes 5 V for the ESP32, the servo and the radar. Every ground is one ground.

TMC2209 on the expansion board

Pot to 0.85 V (pot top to GND), 12 V on, motor unplugged. Driver's EN pin on the expansion board's EN position. Motor order as printed on your expansion board; if it buzzes, swap black and green.

KW12-3 roller switch

COM to the GND rail, NO to GPIO32, NC nothing. On your switch, from the lever's hinge end: COM, NO, NC (read off the part 2026-09-25). Confirm before soldering, meter on beep: at rest COM beeps to NC only; lever held, COM beeps to NO only. The leg that beeps both times is COM. Solder, then heat shrink each leg.

Micro servo

The servo's 3-pin plug has red in the middle. Use three separate jumpers from the plug: orange to GPIO13, red to the 5 V rail, brown to the GND rail. Never red to the ESP32's 3V3 pin.

HLK-LD2450 radar

Pin order per Hi-Link's manual (5V, RX, TX, GND); check the print beside the plug. TX to GPIO16, RX to GPIO17: they cross. Hi-Link asks for a 5 V supply able to give 200 mA; the buck is fine.

MP1584EN buck, fixed 5 V

12 V in, meter on OUT+ and OUT-: it must read 4.95 to 5.05 V. Nothing to adjust. Some modules share one GND pad for IN- and OUT-; read the print. It runs warm at 1 A: keep it in the open, not under the ESP32.

05bProtoboard layout, one quarter

The ESP32 sits on one quarter of the snappable protoboard (17 holes across, 19 down). Underneath, the copper joins the holes in groups of three across, with a 15-hole rail down each side. So each ESP32 pin shares its group with two more holes: a male pin two holes outward is already connected to it, no wire needed. One rail carries 5 V, the other ground. The second quarter, to its right, is the power board (below). The expansion board mounts on its own.

05Soldering, in order

  1. Header pins on the ESP32

    Solder full 15-pin header strips down both sides, pins pointing down from the underside, so every jumper plugs on. If you only do the ones this build uses: VIN, GND, D13, D27, D26, D25, D32 on the top row (the row with VIN), and GND, RX2, TX2 on the other row. Push the strip into a breadboard to hold it straight, solder one pin at each end, check it is square, then the rest. Each joint a shiny cone, no bridges to the next pin.

  2. Legs on the MP1584EN

    Tin the four pads and solder a short bare wire leg (a resistor clipping) into each, pointing down.

  3. Meter the buck before it goes in

    Hold its IN legs on the 12 V adapter's jack terminals (or clip them). Meter on the OUT legs: 4.95 to 5.05 V. Unplug.

  4. Protoboard: sockets, pins, bridges

    Follow the protoboard drawing in section 05b: two 15-pin female sockets for the ESP32, single male pins beside the used signals and on the two rails, and two solder bridges (a blob of solder, or a short bare wire, across the gap between two neighbouring holes) that tie the ESP32's VIN to the 5 V rail and its GND to the GND rail.

  5. Power board

    Build it per section 05c: two screw terminals, the MP1584EN on wire legs, the red 12 V link, three bridges, two male pins. Check it alone on 12 V before anything else is connected.

  6. Power board to ESP32 board

    Two female-female jumpers: power board 5V OUT to the ESP32 board's "5 V in" rail pin, power board GND OUT to its "GND in" rail pin. 12 V OUT terminal to the expansion board's VMOT and GND.

  7. KW12-3 legs

    Heat shrink on, one wire each to COM and NO, solder quickly (a slow iron melts the switch body), shrink. Female dupont ends: COM to the GND rail, NO to the LIMIT pin. Twist the two wires together along their length.

  8. Check with the meter before power

    ESP32 out of its sockets. Continuity mode: 5 V rail to GND rail must NOT beep. Each signal pin to its socket hole must beep. No signal pin may beep to a rail. KW12-3 NO to COM beeps only while the lever is pressed.

06Mechanical assembly

Condensed from doc 05. Pictures are the CAD renders from the repo; new parts in each step are in full colour.

  1. Press the clutch into the spool

    HF0612 into spool_body from the flange side. Test on a spare rod: spins free one way, locks the other. From the plain flange side (it will face the 606ZZ end), free must be counterclockwise. If not, press it out and flip it. This is the Rev C.1 fix.

  2. Ratchet and line

    Ratchet disk on the boss, 3x M3x8. Tie the 6 lb monofilament straight to the barrel with an arbor knot, a drop of CA on it. No elastic, no braid: the mono's own stretch is the shock absorber.

  3. Bearing and motor

    606ZZ into the end plate. The seat is drawn 17.0 mm from 2026-09-27 (it was 16.8, which would not take the bearing even heated); the print comes out 0.1 to 0.2 small, which is the press. On a bracket printed before then, sand the seat with rolled sandpaper until the bearing presses in. Motor on the outside of the motor plate, 4x M2.5x6, wires toward the base.

  4. Coupler

    5 mm side onto the motor shaft until it stops, blue threadlocker, tighten. 6 mm side loose.

  5. Measure, then stack

    Motor plate outer face to the coupler's free end: 30 mm means use spacer_A_6mm as is; 27 to 29 mm add shims. Hold spacer A, spool (ratchet toward the motor), spacer B in line in the frame.

  6. Rod in

    From outside the 606ZZ plate, through everything into the coupler until it bottoms; turn the spool its free way as the rod enters the needles. Threadlocker, tighten. Check: spool free counterclockwise from the 606ZZ end, clockwise drags the motor. End play no more than 0.5 mm.

  7. Servo and finger

    Servo into the tower pocket, spline toward the spool, two tab screws. No horn: the finger presses straight onto the spline later, at 90 degrees, during calibration (section 09), and the horn screw holds it.

  8. Fairlead, flap and switch

    Clear the bore with a 3 mm drill by hand. KW12-3 on the switch plate, roller toward the fairlead, 2x M2 finger-tight. Flap on an M2 bolt (16 to 20 mm), free in the flap, threaded into the body, not clamped. Slide the switch until the roller just touches the flap tail, back off a paper's thickness, tighten. Print the flap from files dated 2026-09-27 or later: the prong beside the line slot is 3.8 mm wide (the earlier 1.2 mm one was too thin). Lift the flap by hand: click at about 2 mm, then it stops flat.

  9. Screw on the fairlead, thread the line

    Fairlead body screwed to the pad: 2x M3 countersunk screws, 8 to 10 mm, from the ceiling side through the two pad holes nearest the base, heads flush. On an already printed bracket, countersink those two holes first by twisting an 8 mm drill bit by hand. No glue. Wind the line counterclockwise from the 606ZZ end, down through the fairlead, into the flap's slot. Below: 8 mm bead, swivel, spider (60 g at most).

  10. Measure the line

    Unwind fully. Barrel knot to bead, line straight but not pulled: that number is Line length on the web page.

  11. Radar mount

    Glue ld2450_fork flat under the servo end of the bracket (CA or epoxy), ears hanging down, as drawn. Put ld2450_cradle's round knuckle between the ears and push an M3 bolt (20 to 25 mm) through, nut on the far side, snug enough to hold an angle. Its open side faces out toward the approach, away from the fairlead end. Slide the LD2450 up into the grooves from the bottom, antenna side against the face plate (the side with the windows), pin-header end last so the headers sit at the bottom, open at the back; one dab of hot glue on the bottom edge. Set the tilt on site: 50 degrees down for your porch (the beam blocks shallower rays).

  12. Controller on the pad

    Board, expansion board and buck on the electronics pad with foam tape or zip ties through the x = -80 holes. Keep the x = -56 holes and the two at the servo end clear: those are the ceiling screws.

07First power-up, one layer at a time

  1. Buck alone

    12 V on, nothing on the 5 V side. Meter reads 4.95 to 5.05 V (fixed 5 V module, nothing to adjust).

  2. Driver current

    Motor unplugged, 12 V on. Meter from the pot's metal top to GND: turn to 0.85 V (that is 0.6 A). Factory setting is about 1.2 V, too high for this motor.

  3. Everything but the motor

    Power off. Plug in the ESP32, servo, switch, radar. 12 V on. The blue LED blinks slowly when armed and ready. The finger servo swings to its lock angle once at start-up: keep fingers clear.

  4. Motor last

    Power off, plug in the motor, power on. Never plug or unplug the motor with 12 V on.

08The web page is the control panel

The board serves its own page. On a phone or PC on the same WiFi, open http://dropspider.local. If that name does not resolve (some Android phones), use the address the board got from the router: type wifi in the Console, or look at the top line of the page (on 2026-09-25 the new board got 192.168.1.138; the router may change it). If the board cannot join the WiFi within 15 s it makes its own network, DropSpider-setup (password dropspider), and the page is at 192.168.4.1.

09Calibrate from the web page, in this order

Bench first: device base-down on the table edge, spool hanging over so the line can drop about 0.7 m. Any bench move disarms the radar on purpose; step 10 re-arms it. Record each result in the commissioning log.

  1. Page loads READY TODAY

    State reads "ready" or "waiting for the doorway to clear". The Console card shows the start-up lines.

  2. Limit switch reads right READY TODAY

    Lift the fairlead flap by hand and let go, watching the status chip change between "reads pressed" and "reads open". If it is backwards, type liminv 1 in the Console. Then set Limit switch fitted to 1 and press Save settings.

  3. Motor turns cleanly READY TODAY

    Hands and line clear. Bench: motor, +1 turn then -1 turn. One smooth turn each way. Stutters or buzzes: this is the logic-level check (open item V1); see doc 02.

  4. Finger angles READY TODAY

    Bench: finger servo. With the servo at 90, press the finger onto the spline pointing at the rod, horn screw in. Nudge until the finger sits level in the teeth: Store as lock. Nudge toward the floor until it is clear of the teeth: Store as release. Save settings.

  5. Rewind direction READY TODAY

    Spider hanging. +1/8: the spool should wind the line up. If the motor turns but the spool slips, set Rewind direction to the other value and try again. If neither way winds, the clutch is still in the Rev C direction: flip it (assembly step 1).

  6. Line length READY TODAY

    Enter the measured number in Line length, Save settings.

  7. Release under load, drop, rewind, lock AFTER TASK 1

    The Rev C.1 sequence: the motor winds the spool back 1/12 turn while the finger swings out, then runs the drop under power, then rewinds until the switch clicks, then eases the spool onto the finger. New settings arrive with it: drop distance (dropmm), drop speed (droprpm), speed-up and slow-down rates (dropacc, dropdec), finger release time (relms). Tests T1 to T8.

  8. Five full drops AFTER TASK 1

    All complete, no tangles, finger always relocks. After the lock the switch reads open: correct by design, the firmware remembers the spider is home from the last cycle.

  9. Radar live view AFTER TASK 2

    See section 10. Walk toward the door, away, and across; confirm it fires only walking in.

  10. Arm and save READY TODAY

    Press Arm, then Save settings so it stays armed after a power cut.

  • On the porch ceiling, behind the front beam: spider line 305 mm (12 in) behind the beam, centred on the walkway.
  • Rod parallel to the beam. Servo end (radar) toward the beam, fairlead end away from it.
  • 4 screws: the 2 at the servo end, the 2 pad holes at x = -56. Joist: #4 x 1 in wood screws. Drywall only: ribbed anchors or small toggles.
  • LD2450: on the device, in its tilting cradle under the servo end, looking out from under the porch at people walking in; tilt 50 degrees down for your porch (8 ft ceiling, beam 10 in deep, device 12 in behind it), fine-tuned in the walk test. Mount the device with its servo end toward the porch's open side. Upright (manual figure 6). Check on the bench: walk across in front of it and X must change. Lead about 10 cm to the controller.
  • Not through metal. The radar sees through wood, drywall and plastic, but aluminum siding or trim blocks it completely, however thin. If the beam is clad in aluminum, the radar only sees people under the beam's bottom edge, and the metal can bounce back phantom targets. Tilt steeper if it fires late or fires on nothing.
  • 12 V adapter stays at an outdoor-safe outlet; DC extension up the post. Keep the electronics out of blowing rain.

Line length for your ceiling

line = (ceiling - 85) - (rest height + spider height + 30) + 45

All in mm. Example: (2440 - 85) - (1550 + 100 + 30) + 45 = 720. Tie it long, enter it as Line length, then shorten on site until the resting height is right.

12On site, then arm

  1. Five drops in place AFTER TASK 1

    Adjust Line length (and drop distance) until the spider stops at face height.

  2. Walk tests AFTER TASK 2

    Walk in from 3 m: it fires and the spider arrives as you reach the doorway. Walk away, walk across, stand still: no drop.

  3. No double scare READY TODAY

    After a scare, stand in the doorway: nothing until you leave and 20 s pass (Lockout after a scare).

  4. Before each night

    Check the knots, bead, flap and the line for nicks. Soft foam spider only, 60 g at most. Press Disarm, or unplug, when small children are about to run through.
