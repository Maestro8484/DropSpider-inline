DropSpider build guide, Rev C.1

One page, top to bottom: wire the 30-pin ESP32 DevKit V1, assemble the device, power it up, then set it up and test it from its own web page. It covers the install you are building: the device turned over on two steel corner braces on the porch beam's inside face. The ceiling install is in doc 06 only. The repo docs (02, 05, 06, 07, 09) are the source of truth; if this page disagrees, the doc wins.

The board runs the Rev C.1 firmware (flashed 2026-09-27): motor-led drop, release under load, seat move on lock. None of it has been watched working with the motor and the spool fitted yet (tests T1 to T8); run section 09 on the bench before anything goes overhead.

01Parts and tools

Electrical parts (all already owned)

  • 30-pin ESP32 DevKit V1 (the 38-pin NodeMCU-32S works too, same GPIO numbers)
  • TMC2209 driver on the expansion board
  • NEMA 11 stepper motor, 4 wires
  • Micro servo (yours is an MG90, about 90 degrees of swing, checked 2026-09-27)
  • KW12-3 roller micro switch
  • HLK-LD2450 radar with its 4-pin 1.25 mm cable
  • MP1584EN mini buck module, fixed 5 V out
  • 12 V 2 A adapter with screw-terminal jack
  • Dupont jumpers, 22 AWG wire for the 12 V run, heat shrink

Left out on purpose: the 470 uF servo capacitor, the 74AHCT125 buffer and the LD2410C backup radar. Each is only fitted if a named test fails.

The two bearings: do not mix them up

  • HF0612 one-way bearing (also called the clutch): 6 mm bore, 10 mm outside, 12 mm long, needle rollers inside. Spins free one way and locks the other. Presses into spool_body. This is what lets the spool run down behind the motor on the drop without ever overrunning it.
  • 606ZZ ball bearing: 6 mm bore, 17 mm outside, spins both ways. Presses into the bracket's end plate and carries the far end of the rod.

Tools

  • Multimeter (needed before anything is connected)
  • Soldering iron, solder, heat gun or lighter for heat shrink
  • Small Phillips, small flat screwdriver (driver current pot)
  • 1.5 mm and 2 mm hex keys (coupler set screws)
  • Vise or clamp, CA glue (line knot only), blue threadlocker
  • Drill with a 3.4 mm and a 7 mm (or 1/4 in) bit: five holes in the base
  • Hot glue gun: expansion board and buck onto the pad, nothing else
  • A phone or PC on the same WiFi as the board

02Board pinout, top view

Board face up, antenna end away from you, USB toward you. This is the 30-pin ESP32 DevKit V1: 15 pins a side, printed D25 for GPIO25 and so on, RX2/TX2 for GPIO16/17, VIN for the 5 V input. Go by the name printed next to the pin on your board, not by counting. On the 38-pin NodeMCU-32S the same GPIO numbers sit in other places.

03Power: one tree, no loops

12 V feeds the motor driver and the buck converter. The buck makes 5 V for the ESP32, the servo and the radar. Every ground is one ground.

TMC2209 on the expansion board

Pot to 0.85 V (pot top to GND), 12 V on, motor unplugged. Driver's EN pin on the expansion board's EN position. Motor order as printed on your expansion board; if it buzzes, swap black and green.

KW12-3 roller switch

COM to the GND rail, NO to GPIO32, NC nothing. On your switch, from the lever's hinge end: COM, NO, NC (read off the part 2026-09-25). Confirm before soldering, meter on beep: at rest COM beeps to NC only; lever held, COM beeps to NO only. The leg that beeps both times is COM. Solder, then heat shrink each leg.

Micro servo

The servo's 3-pin plug has red in the middle. Use three separate jumpers from the plug: orange to GPIO13, red to the 5 V rail, brown to the GND rail. Never red to the ESP32's 3V3 pin.

HLK-LD2450 radar

Pin order per Hi-Link's manual (5V, RX, TX, GND); check the print beside the plug. TX to GPIO16, RX to GPIO17: they cross. Hi-Link asks for a 5 V supply able to give 200 mA; the buck is fine.

MP1584EN buck, fixed 5 V

12 V in, meter on OUT+ and OUT-: it must read 4.95 to 5.05 V. Nothing to adjust. Some modules share one GND pad for IN- and OUT-; read the print. It runs warm at 1 A: keep it in the open, not under the ESP32.

05bProtoboard layout, one quarter

The ESP32 sits on one quarter of the snappable protoboard (17 holes across, 19 down). Underneath, the copper joins the holes in groups of three across, with a 15-hole rail down each side. So each ESP32 pin shares its group with two more holes: a male pin two holes outward is already connected to it, no wire needed. One rail carries 5 V, the other ground. This is the only protoboard: the expansion board and the MP1584EN buck are hot-glued beside it on the pad, each on its own wires.

Check before soldering: drop the ESP32's header pins into the board. They must land in columns 3 and 13, 10 holes apart. That spacing is read off your photo, not measured; if your pins land 9 holes apart, use columns 3 and 12 and move the right-hand pins one column in.

05Soldering, in order

  1. Header pins on the ESP32

    Solder full 15-pin header strips down both sides, pins pointing down from the underside, so every jumper plugs on. If you only do the ones this build uses: VIN, GND, D13, D27, D26, D25, D32 on the top row (the row with VIN), and GND, RX2, TX2 on the other row. Push the strip into a breadboard to hold it straight, solder one pin at each end, check it is square, then the rest. Each joint a shiny cone, no bridges to the next pin.

  2. Wires on the MP1584EN

    Tin the four pads. IN+ and IN-: two 22 AWG wires long enough to reach the 12 V jack. OUT+ and OUT-: two wires ending in female dupont plugs for the protoboard's "5 V in" and "GND in" pins. Heat shrink every joint.

  3. Meter the buck before it goes in

    IN wires into the 12 V jack's screw terminals. Meter on the OUT wires: 4.95 to 5.05 V. Unplug.

  4. Protoboard: sockets, pins, bridges

    Follow the protoboard drawing in section 05b: two 15-pin female sockets for the ESP32, single male pins beside the used signals and on the two rails, and two solder bridges (a blob of solder, or a short bare wire, across the gap between two neighbouring holes) that tie the ESP32's VIN to the 5 V rail and its GND to the GND rail.

  5. 12 V and 5 V wiring

    The jack's screw terminals take two wires each: + to the expansion board's VMOT and to the buck's IN+, - to the expansion board's GND and to the buck's IN-, 22 AWG. The buck's OUT+ and OUT- plug onto the protoboard's "5 V in" and "GND in" rail pins.

  6. KW12-3 legs

    Heat shrink on, one wire each to COM and NO, solder quickly (a slow iron melts the switch body), shrink. Female dupont ends: COM to the GND rail, NO to the LIMIT pin. Twist the two wires together along their length.

  7. Check with the meter before power

    ESP32 out of its sockets. Continuity mode: 5 V rail to GND rail must NOT beep. Each signal pin to its socket hole must beep. No signal pin may beep to a rail. KW12-3 NO to COM beeps only while the lever is pressed.

06Mechanical assembly

Condensed from doc 05. Pictures are the CAD renders from the repo; new parts in each step are in full colour.

  1. Press the one-way bearing into the spool

    The HF0612 one-way bearing (the clutch; the small 10 mm one with needle rollers, not the 606ZZ) into spool_body from the flange side, with a vise or clamp. The hole is drawn 10.7 mm so it prints as a tight press you can still push in (the old 10.0 hole would not take it: reprint spool_body and spool_ratchet, whose center hole is now 11.0 mm so the bearing's stub slips in without sanding). Test on a spare rod: spins free one way, locks the other. From the plain flange side (it will face the 606ZZ end), free must be counterclockwise. If not, press it out and flip it. This is the Rev C.1 fix.

  2. Ratchet and line

    Ratchet disk on the boss, 3x M3x8. Tie the 6 lb monofilament straight to the barrel with an arbor knot, a drop of CA on it. No elastic, no braid: the mono's own stretch is the shock absorber.

  3. Ball bearing and motor

    606ZZ ball bearing (the 17 mm one) into the end plate. The seat is drawn 17.0 mm from 2026-09-27 (it was 16.8, which would not take the bearing even heated); the print comes out 0.1 to 0.2 small, which is the press. On a bracket printed before then, sand the seat with rolled sandpaper until the bearing presses in. Motor on the outside of the motor plate, 4x M2.5x6, wires toward the base.

  4. Coupler

    5 mm side onto the motor shaft until it stops, blue threadlocker, tighten. 6 mm side loose.

  5. Measure, then stack

    Motor plate outer face to the coupler's free end: 30 mm means use spacer_A_6mm as is; 27 to 29 mm add shims. Hold spacer A, spool (ratchet toward the motor), spacer B in line in the frame.

  6. Rod in

    From outside the 606ZZ plate, through everything into the coupler until it bottoms; turn the spool its free way as the rod enters the needles. Threadlocker, tighten. Check: spool free counterclockwise from the 606ZZ end, clockwise drags the motor. End play no more than 0.5 mm.

  7. Servo and finger

    Servo into the tower pocket, spline toward the spool, two tab screws. No horn: the finger presses straight onto the spline later, at 90 degrees, during calibration (section 09), and the horn screw holds it.

  8. Printed parts for this install

    fairlead_base and fairlead_flap_inv (fairlead v2, 2026-09-28: the switch sits above the flap, the flap is the lowest point; the older fairlead_flap has no tab and does not fit), ld2450_fork_screw and ld2450_cradle. fairlead_body is the ceiling install's and is not used.

  9. Holes in the base

    A bracket printed from bracket.stl dated 2026-09-28 or later has all five holes printed in: the 7 mm line pass-through and four 3.4 mm screw holes. Skip this step. Only on an older print: print cad/bracket_footprint_inverted.svg at 100% (its 100 mm bar must measure 100), lay it on the base's flat outer face, printed side up, lined up with the base's outline, and drill through the blue crosses. The dashed outlines show where the fairlead block and the radar fork will sit; the block sits beside the long cutout under the spool, not over it. Five holes: 7 mm for the line (a 1/4 in bit is close enough), 3.4 mm x2 for the fairlead block (the pair nearest the spool on the servo side), 3.4 mm x2 for the radar fork (at the servo end). Sand the 7 mm hole smooth inside and on both edges: the line runs through it.

  10. Switch and flap onto the block

    Solder the switch wires first. Clear the block's bore with a 3 mm drill by hand. KW12-3 on the block's outer side (away from the rod), lever and roller down, legs up: 2x M2 x 12 screws through the switch, threading straight into the plastic (no nuts), snug. Flap on an M2 bolt, 20 mm, in from the rod side through the knuckle and threaded into the hinge boss, not clamped; its tab goes under the roller. At rest the flap hangs a little nose-down and the roller sits clear of the tab. Lift the flap's free end by hand: it touches the roller, clicks, then stops flat against the block.

  11. Block onto the base

    Flat face of the block against the base's outer face, its bore under the 7 mm hole. 2x M3 x 12 from inside the base (heads on the device side), cutting their own thread into the block. Snug, not tight: the thread is plastic. Look down through the 7 mm hole: you must see straight through the bore. No glue.

  12. Thread the line

    Wind the line counterclockwise seen from the 606ZZ end, and lead it off the spool's servo side, down through the 7 mm hole and the block's bore, then slide it into the flap's slot from the flap's free end. Below: 8 mm bead, swivel, spider (60 g at most).

  13. Measure the line

    Unwind fully. Barrel knot to bead, line straight but not pulled: that number is Line length on the web page.

  14. Check by hand

    Pull the bead up against the flap: the flap swings toward the base, the switch clicks, the flap stops flat against the block. That is "spider home". Let go: the flap drops back to its rest stop and the switch opens.

  15. Radar mount

    No glue. ld2450_fork_screw under the base at the servo end, ears hanging down: 2x M3 x 10 up through the fork and the base, nuts inside. Put ld2450_cradle's round knuckle between the ears and push an M3 bolt (25 to 30 mm) through, nut on the far side, snug enough to hold an angle. The cradle's open side faces back toward the beam. Slide the LD2450 up into the grooves from the bottom, antenna side against the face plate (the side with the windows), pin-header end last so the headers sit at the bottom, open at the back; a small zip tie round the cradle's bottom end keeps it from sliding out. Tilt 50 degrees down to start; set it on site (section 11).

  16. Controller on the pad

    The pad is the flat area at the motor-plate end (x -88 to -36), on the device side of the base, now facing up. The protoboard with the ESP32 on it, foam tape or zip ties through the x = -80 holes. The expansion board and the buck hot-glued beside it, clear of the spool. Switch and radar leads run round the base's far (servo) end and back along the top to the board, about 15 cm and 25 cm, tied to the base so the spool cannot catch them.

07First power-up, one layer at a time

  1. Buck alone

    12 V on, nothing on the 5 V side. Meter reads 4.95 to 5.05 V (fixed 5 V module, nothing to adjust).

  2. Driver current

    Motor unplugged, 12 V on. Meter from the pot's metal top to GND: turn to 0.85 V (that is 0.6 A). Factory setting is about 1.2 V, too high for this motor.

  3. Everything but the motor

    Power off. Plug in the ESP32, servo, switch, radar. 12 V on. The blue LED blinks slowly when armed and ready. The finger servo swings to its lock angle once at start-up: keep fingers clear.

  4. Motor last

    Power off, plug in the motor, power on. Never plug or unplug the motor with 12 V on.

08The web page is the control panel

The board serves its own page. On a phone or PC on the same WiFi, open http://dropspider.local. If that name does not resolve (some Android phones), use the address the board got from the router: type wifi in the Console, or look at the top line of the page (on 2026-09-25 the new board got 192.168.1.138; the router may change it). If the board cannot join the WiFi within 15 s it makes its own network, DropSpider-setup (password dropspider), and the page is at 192.168.4.1.

What the chips mean: armed means a radar signal may fire it. Nothing drives the radar input today (the LD2450 needs task 2; the LD2410C backup is not fitted), so the radar chip always reads clear and only Drop it now or the BOOT button fire it. Motor powered appears whenever the driver is on. Spider home is the limit switch. A red chip is a fault, with its reason; three in a row and it stops until you press Clear fault.

09Calibrate from the web page, in this order

Bench first: device base-down on the table, slid out over the edge until the fairlead block and the radar under the base hang free, held or clamped, so the line can drop about 0.7 m to the floor. Any bench move disarms the radar on purpose; step 10 re-arms it. Record each result in the commissioning log.

  1. Page loads READY TODAY

    State reads "ready" or "waiting for the doorway to clear". The Console card shows the start-up lines.

  2. Limit switch reads right READY TODAY

    Lift the fairlead flap by hand and let go, watching the status chip change between "reads pressed" and "reads open". If it is backwards, type liminv 1 in the Console. Then set Limit switch fitted to 1 and press Save settings.

  3. Motor turns cleanly READY TODAY

    Hands and line clear. Bench: motor, +1 turn then -1 turn. One smooth turn each way. Stutters or buzzes: this is the logic-level check (open item V1); see doc 02.

  4. Finger angles READY TODAY

    Set 2026-09-27 and saved: lock 85, release 50 (finger on the spline, ratchet not yet on the rod). Recheck with the ratchet in place: Go to lock angle, the finger must sit level in the teeth; Go to release angle, it must be clear of them. If not, nudge, Store as lock or Store as release, Save settings.

  5. Rewind direction READY TODAY

    Spider hanging. +1/8: the spool should wind the line up. If the motor turns but the spool slips, set Rewind direction to the other value and try again. If neither way winds, the HF0612 one-way bearing is still in the Rev C direction: flip it (assembly step 1).

  6. Line length READY TODAY

    First set Drop distance to no more than the line minus 102 mm (636 line: 534), or the page refuses the new line: the factory drop, 620, only fits the old 720 line. Then enter the measured number in Line length, Save settings.

  7. Release under load, drop, rewind, lock READY TODAY

    The Rev C.1 sequence: the motor winds the spool back 1/12 turn while the finger swings out, then runs the drop under power, then rewinds until the switch clicks, then eases the spool onto the finger. Not yet run with the motor and spool fitted. Its settings: drop distance (dropmm), drop speed (droprpm), speed-up and slow-down rates (dropacc, dropdec), finger release time (relms). Tests T1 to T8.

  8. Five full drops READY TODAY

    All complete, no tangles, finger always relocks. After the lock the switch reads open: correct by design, the firmware remembers the spider is home from the last cycle.

  9. Radar live view AFTER TASK 2

    See section 10. Walk toward the door, away, and across; confirm it fires only walking in.

  10. Arm and save READY TODAY

    Press Arm, then Save settings so it stays armed after a power cut.

Your plan, approved 2026-09-27, doc 06. The device upside down, base at the bottom, sitting on the flat legs of two 6 in steel corner braces whose other leg stands up the beam's inside face. Checked in the CAD model; not yet built or hung.

  • Braces: yours to place and drill. Upright legs up the beam's inside face, tops about 8 mm under the ceiling; flat legs under the base. A bolt through the base is clear between 24 and 55 mm from the rod's centre line on the servo side, at either end of the base; at the motor end also within 12 mm of the rod. Never within 20 mm of the rod at the 606ZZ end: the bearing plate stands there.
  • Rod parallel to the beam, the pad's edge (the electronics side) against the beam. The line falls 117 mm out from the beam's face, centred on the walkway.
  • LD2450: under the base at the servo end, looking back toward the beam and down. Start at 50 degrees; if it fires late or fires on nothing, go to 55 or 60 (the cradle clears to 65). Check on the bench: walk across in front of it and X must change.
  • Not through metal. The radar sees through wood, drywall and plastic, but aluminum siding or trim blocks it completely, however thin. If the beam is clad in aluminum, the radar only sees people under the beam's bottom edge, and the metal can bounce back phantom targets. Tilt steeper.
  • The spider hangs inside the radar's view. If the radar counts it as a person, the device would sit waiting for the doorway to clear after each scare; the first walk test shows it.
  • 12 V adapter stays at an outdoor-safe outlet; DC extension up the post. Keep the electronics out of blowing rain.

Line length for your porch

line = (ceiling - 196) - (rest height + spider height + 30) + 72

All in mm. Example: (2440 - 196) - (1550 + 100 + 30) + 72 = 636. Tie it long, enter it as Line length, then shorten on site until the resting height is right. Order on the day: device finished on the bench (section 06), braces to the beam, device onto their flat legs, bolted through your holes.

12On site, then arm

  1. Five drops in place READY TODAY

    Adjust Line length (and drop distance) until the spider stops at face height.

  2. Walk tests AFTER TASK 2

    Walk in from 3 m: it fires and the spider arrives as you reach the doorway. Walk away, walk across, stand still: no drop.

  3. No double scare READY TODAY

    After a scare, stand in the doorway: nothing until you leave and 20 s pass (Lockout after a scare).

  4. Before each night

    Check the knots, bead, flap and the line for nicks. Soft foam spider only, 60 g at most. Press Disarm, or unplug, when small children are about to run through.
