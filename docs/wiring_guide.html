4. Parts to leave out: over-engineering check

PartCallWhy
470 uF capacitor at the servo SKIP A micro servo stalls at well under 1 A, and it moves for a fraction of a second per scare. The buck converter already has its own output capacitors, and the ESP32 has its own 3.3 V regulator after VIN. Fit one only if the board restarts when the finger moves (the page's "Running for" clock resets). A restart does no damage: settings are kept, and the board never rewinds on its own at start-up, so the worst case is a spider left hanging that you rewind from the page.
74AHCT125 logic buffer ONLY IF Only if the motor stutters or will not turn on jog 1600 (commissioning step 4, item V1). Most TMC2209 boards take 3.3 V signals directly.
LD2410C backup radar SKIP The C4002 carries its own backup: its OUT pin on GPIO33. The LD2410C would need that same pin, so it stays in the drawer.
Resistors or capacitors on the limit switch SKIP The chip's own pull-up and the firmware's debounce (ignoring the contact's first few milliseconds of chatter) cover it. Twisting the switch's two wires together is enough if the motor wires run beside them.
Bigger power adapter SKIP The 12 V 2 A adapter has three times the headroom the motor needs.
Capacitor at the driver's 12 V input NEEDED Not over-engineering: a stepper driver can be killed by a voltage spike on 12 V without one. Most expansion boards already have one (a round can near the power terminal). Look for it; if yours has one, nothing to add. Not checked on this expansion board.
Metering the buck (5.0 V) and setting the driver to 0.85 V first NEEDED The buck is a fixed 5 V MP1584EN, but a faulty or mislabelled module would kill the ESP32, servo and radar. The driver's factory current is too high for this motor.

5. Order to wire and power up

  1. Buck on 12 V alone: meter it, 4.95 to 5.05 V.
  2. Driver on 12 V, motor unplugged: set 0.85 V on the pot.
  3. Power off. Wire the ESP32, servo, switch and radar. Motor still unplugged.
  4. Power on: web page at dropspider.local shows "ready". Lift the switch flap and watch it change.
  5. Power off, plug in the motor, power on, then jog 1600 with hands clear.
